## C. The adoption boundary for edits and evidence

| Operation | Before Keep (recommended, staged or not kept) | After Keep |
|---|---|---|
| Edit (note, mark, plan title, context, publish) | Refused. MCP names `keep_recommendation` or `keep_ensemble`; the web returns 409, or 400 for a plan title | Works |
| Comment | Refused (MCP; web 409) | Works |
| Check-in | Refused (`log_visit`; web 400) | Works |
| Ownership | Refused (`record_note_ownership`; web 409) | Works |
| Warrant | Refused (MCP; web 409) | Works |

**A refused attempt writes nothing** (R3): no evidence, no Adoption, no change to the record, and no provenance row.

**When the member's own words carry the intent**, the assistant keeps the record, then records the relationship ("I went there last year", "I bought that coffee", "I stand behind it"):
- `keep_recommendation` and the server instructions say this, "without asking again". Praise alone doesn't count.
- `add_travel_mark` with `visited_on` on a recommended place does both, in order (R6).
- History reads *adoption (recommendation)* → *relationship* (R5).

**Ownership:**
- New "owned but not kept" states can't be created any more. Ownership of a staged Note is refused, so a discard can no longer leave one behind (E9, T8b).
- **Historical data can hold them.** Before 052, a Note staged in a composition still pending review could have been marked owned. The 052 backfill leaves such a Note un-kept, explained by its pending composition. It exists only if production has one, and I can't tell from here. Check after deploy with:
  `SELECT o.uid FROM objects o JOIN ownership_assertions a ON a.note_uid=o.uid WHERE NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM adopted_objects x WHERE x.id=o.id);`
- Legacy rows like these keep decision A's survival rule, which is still tested with planted rows (T9, T9b).
- **Not guarded, on purpose:** withdrawals on existing evidence (release or correct ownership, revoke a warrant, edit or delete a check-in). They only reduce what's already there, and on an un-kept record they can apply only to legacy rows.

## D. Prospective composites

- **Recommended itinerary:**
  - Its composition stays open: days, new places, stop times, stop notes, reordering and stop removal (P5, E5, L8b).
  - Nothing in it is adopted until the plan is kept. Keeping it adopts the plan, its places and the notes under its stops (P6, R14).
  - Retitling or publishing it is refused before Keep (E3).
- **Pending ensemble:**
  - Composing it with recommended pieces, and identifying a piece, adopts nothing (P1).
  - Keeping one recommended piece individually adopts that piece only (P2).
  - Discarding the unkept composition adopts nothing: the kept piece stays Kept, the other recommendation stays prospective, and the staged piece is cleaned up (P3).
  - Keeping the composition adopts its recommended piece and the piece it identified, reuses a kept piece without re-recording it, and cites the Ensemble (P4).
- **Child Notes and Marks:** composition never adopts them. Parent Keep does, and so does an explicit individual Keep.
- **Gap, non-blocking:** a piece identified *during* a pending composition, not a recommendation, has no individual Keep in MCP. It becomes the member's when they keep the composition. That's the general-Keep work in §K.

## E. Parent/child lifecycle

- **Parent adoption propagates:** P4, P6, L2a, L2e, L7b.
- **Each child then has its own lifecycle:** L2b, L2c and L2f (the child survives), L3b (a pre-existing child), L4 (the child is used by another parent).
- **Parent deletion takes no check-in, ownership, warrant or comment** from the children it introduced (L5).
- **A child is removed only by its own deletion,** recorded as the member's act (L6).
- **Child deletion:** the same Stop or component de-resolves in place (D1–D5, D13–D16). Stable uids, day, order, times, notes and lineage survive.
- **Re-resolution:**
  - A later record with the same name is never bound automatically (D8, D20).
  - Normal resolution works and appends to the history: created → de_resolved → resolved (D9, D21).

## F. Recommendation target deletion

**Where it's implemented:** in migration 054, which isn't shipped yet, so it was extended in place. Triggers on deleting a Note, Mark or Itinerary.

**What happens to the Recommendation:**
- It survives. `target_type` and `target_uid` are cleared, and `resolution` stays `resolved`: it *was* resolved.
- Known details stay. Only empty fields are filled, from the record being deleted: a Note's link and picture; a Mark's name, locality, country, address and position.
- A `de_resolved` provenance row (system, cascade) names the deleted target, and the earlier history is untouched.

**Later resolution:**
- `resolve_recommendation` with a target binds it again explicitly (T6).
- `keep_recommendation` runs the normal identity resolution at Keep time. It finds an existing record of theirs, or makes one from what the recommendation knows, then keeps it (T7), with `enriched` provenance naming where the record came from.
- A deleted *plan* isn't remade as an empty plan: Keep refuses (T8).

**Never done:** no auto-binding to a same-name record (T5), no replacement record until Keep, and no Adoption.

**Also covered:**
- The backfill clears references that were already dangling.
- A place made for a never-kept recommended plan that was later deleted stays explained by that Recommendation's history (no orphans, T8 and I1). That place is left in place, not cleaned up, as you decided.

**Schema change:** triggers only, in 054. No new columns and no MCP schema change. `target` is already nullable.

## G. Privacy

**The leak fixed earlier in this pass:** a *private* Note's uid reached other members through a *public* composition's component history and image lineage. `noteUidFor` now reveals a uid only to a viewer who can see that Note, or to the owner.

**Regression coverage:**

- D18: after the Note is deleted, and so de-resolved;

**Snapshots on de-resolution** copy a name or picture into a public parent only when that Note was public and kept (D15). A Stop whose private Mark was failing closed stays hidden (D22).
